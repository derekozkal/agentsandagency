Agents and Agency · No. 1

Our Perfect Future

Terms and conditions may apply

By Derek Özkal · October 1, 2025 · Originally published on Substack

I hold two competing thoughts at once.

AI is magical. If you spend enough time with AI tools, at some point you will see something akin to a magic trick1. (An illusion, Michael!) For me, it was when I uploaded a picture of all the fun little plugs and wires on the back of my receiver, subwoofer, and TV to ChatGPT. It told me how to connect everything and then troubleshoot it successfully when it didn’t work the first time. It was super-fast and just worked.2 That is magic. Let’s put a pin in the conversation about AI being a magician that can’t reveal how the trick is done. For this point, focus on AI’s ability to match expert humans at a growing list of tasks (well beyond audio wiring) in a fraction of the time.

AI is a wrecking ball. It’s probably already knocking out the bottom rungs of the career ladder3 and it’s definitely consuming our collective attention. Between November 2022 when ChatGPT-3 was launched and July 2025, ChatGPT usage has grown in an unprecedented fashion with 18 billion messages sent weekly by more than 700 million users.4 I think that is the same number of commercials where companies blather on about AI-powered whatever for you, the consumer. Whether we ever reach AGI or ASI may be beside the point; today’s systems are already reshaping so many things: where we turn to for information, creative thinking, analysis, education, work, workers, our opinion of the em-dash, oh, and maybe even what it means to be human.

Love it or hate it5, AI is the latest technological inflection point for humanity.6 We usually emerge richer as a society, yet the gains rarely reach everyone. The dynamism that powers innovation often brings strife to those who are disrupted. I might write about whether this time is different at some point in the future, but mostly I plan to write at least a little bit ahead of where we are right now. This means I’m going to get stuff wrong. I’ll try to write based on the most readily available, high-quality research and data available, but data and research lags. So understanding the right now can be challenging and then trying to figure out where we will be in the next few years is orders of magnitude harder. But it’s going to be fun to try!

Wait, what is this?

I’m not sure yet, but here are some thoughts.

One goal for this effort is to translate rigorous research into clear stories and policy insights that protect dignity, agency, and purpose for workers. I write to highlight practical levers that policymakers, educators, and local leaders can pull today, long before AI’s future becomes fully realized. Perhaps more importantly, I hope this will also be a bit of a guide for others trying to figure out their journey in this new AI world.

I write to keep human dignity, judgment, and meaning at the center of every AI debate. Over the past decade I have turned academic work on technology, labor, and entrepreneurship into presentations, policy memos, grant recommendations, and plain-language explainers for civic leaders. Translating those ideas into a steady public voice outside my day job has been harder than I expected. I draft, pause, revise, and lose momentum. I’m optimistic that publishing this first entry will hold me accountable.

Ok, but seriously what are you going to write about?

Yet again, I’m not sure, but here are some thoughts! These are serious topics, but I also hope to entertain with my writing because, well, humor always helps me.7

Topic: Work teams in the age of AI

Working Title: “Designing Work Teams Comprised of Humans and AI Agents”

As AI Agent capabilities grow, we may not be far away from the day that work teams and departments are comprised of four types of workers: human employees, human independent contractors, firm-owned AI agents, and external AI agents (the AI agent equivalent of independent contractors). If that’s confusing, that’s totally fair. I’m not certain this isn’t an oversimplification, but maybe this table helps.

Mostly Full Firm ControlLimited Firm Control
HumansEmployeesIndependent Contractors
AI AgentsFirm-Owned AI AgentsExternal AI Agents

For example, a marketing team might use its own “Firm-Owned” agent trained on internal sales data while hiring an “External” specialized legal-review agent from a third-party vendor for a specific campaign. Anyway, in the near future, how will we structure jobs, work duties, career progression and more when teams are part human and part AI agents. How will we design AI agents for work? How should we? How different will the answers to those questions be? Are there lessons from the emergence of the Internet and social media?

Topic: Opportunity

Working Title: “Solo, Not Alone: Agent-Powered Micro-Firms”

When AI agents do the entry-level grunt work, will it become more likely that your first job will be self-employment? I will outline a blueprint for one-person businesses that bundle agents for sales, ops, and bookkeeping without sacrificing craft or dignity. This will also include the policy guardrails (portable benefits, fair credit) that let these firms scale.

Topic: Agency

Working Title: “Civic Agent Labs”

If apprenticeships and junior level positions vanish, where do people learn judgment before wielding powerful tools? This article proposes publicly funded “agent labs” where anyone can practice steering AI on low-risk civic tasks, supported by psychological-safety design and stipend models. It offers a practical roadmap for governments and philanthropies to cultivate real human agency in an agent-driven world.

Will I Use AI to Write This?

A little bit, maybe. For now, I’m planning on writing this all myself first8 and then maybe using AI as an editor. Maybe I could train an AI agent to write like this for me, but I’d like to delay that future and it seems easiest to do that by keeping my writing here and my AI usage mostly separate.

So, what’s next?

The end… of this blog introduction, not the world… probably.

If these questions about work, dignity, and our AI future are on your mind too, consider subscribing for future posts.

Thanks for reading!
