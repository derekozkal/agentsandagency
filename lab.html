The Lab

I write about AI and work. Here I build with it.

Building is how I check whether the ideas hold up. Everything here is a prototype. Some are tools, some are models, and three are games.

Ground rule: anything marked Prototype is an argument I haven't verified yet. If a number or coefficient looks wrong, tell me in the comments.

The AI Diffusion Atlas

Who is actually adopting AI in America: businesses, workers, and states, built entirely on published data from the Census Bureau, the Federal Reserve, and the Anthropic Economic Index. The two adoption numbers people quote most (18% of firms, 41% of workers) are both right, and the Atlas shows why they differ.

Exhibit BLive · featured by the Ewing Marion Kauffman Foundation

The Legacy Project

Sixty years of foundation history was sitting in boxes, binders, and file shares that nobody could search. I led 46 volunteers in digitizing it, then helped design an AI research tool integrated with our grant-management software, so the archive answers questions instead of gathering dust.

Staff can use it to pull institutional context in seconds: what we funded, what we learned, what we already tried in 1997. Information retrieval that took days now takes minutes, and the tool opened working capacity and possibilities for staff that didn't exist before.

It also convinced me that the Expertise Encoder role in Exhibit D is real work, because I did a version of it here.

Exhibit CPrototype · a toy model, on purpose

The Expertise Flow Simulator

My essay The Liquidity Crisis of Expertise argues that a profession can run out of usable judgment the way a bank runs out of cash: solvent on paper, short when it counts. This simulator turns that claim into a model with three sliders, one profession, and a 25-year horizon.

Model

One profession, 25 years, year 0 = 100

How fast the profession hands cognitive work to models. Speeds up encoding, and quietly starves the junior pipeline.

Deliberate spending on the slow way: juniors doing hard things next to seniors.

How much of a retiring expert's judgment actually survives capture. Less than you'd hope.

Total capacityHeld by people (tacit)Captured in AI (encoded)

How it works, in one breath. Three stocks: judgment held by working seniors, judgment captured into systems, and the junior pipeline. Retirement drains the first at 5% a year, encoding takes about a decade to mature, and your sliders control the rest. Toys make assumptions visible. If you'd set a coefficient differently, tell me which one and why.

Exhibit EVerified by consumption · replication ongoing

The Kitchen

I spend my working hours on human-AI collaboration, so it seems only fair to disclose my own. These were made by me, with AI as sous-chef for recipes, ratios, and troubleshooting. Peer review was conducted by my family, and the braided cinnamon apple bread got a "revise and resubmit." So I made an apple pie, which went much better.

Findings so far: AI is a good cooking partner. It's patient with basic questions, happy to help at 10 p.m., and wrong about an oven temperature about once per project. Also in the rotation: veggie burgers, stews, and a cheesecake. Methodology shared on request.

The Arcade

Three browser games built with AI. All are free, need no account, and work on a phone.

New

Herd Mentality

A herd of laser-horned unicorns defends a rainbow bridge from the Grumps, an army of gray clouds. Drag to steer, shoot gates to raise their numbers, and run through the better one. A Grump you hit floats away happy. Bosses include The Monday and Per My Last Email. Levels don't end, and progress saves in your browser.

Play Herd Mentality

Math Grove

A summer math tutor built for my daughters and open to anyone. Solve problems, earn coins, and grow a garden that's all yours. Two grade bridges, entering 3rd grade and entering 6th grade, each with 8 topics and 3 tiers. Clear all three tiers to earn a star. There's also a bonus Snake game played inside the garden you grew. No account, no ads, saves in your browser.

Open Math Grove

Directorate

Run a covert agency across four eras, 1972 to 2026. Build headquarters, work field leads, solve conspiracies before they execute, and manage global tension. Eight minigames feed a strategy layer. Influenced by some of my favorite games as a kid, like Covert Action, SimCity, Conflict, and Age of Empires II. One sitting, no save state.
