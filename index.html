The Legacy Project

Led by Derek Ozkal, senior officer, strategic initiatives, RLE

From A project to learn from, and honor, 60 years of the Ewing Marion Kauffman Foundation, the Foundation's account of the work.

Sixty years of foundation history sat in boxes, binders, and file shares that nobody could search. I led 46 volunteers in turning tens of thousands of pages into a secure, searchable archive. Then I helped design an experimental AI tool so staff can ask the archive questions.

Finding what we funded, what we learned, and what we already tried in 1997 now takes minutes instead of days.

About

From Nevada, Missouri

I grew up in Nevada, Missouri (pronounced Knee-vay-duh, because it's a rural Missouri town).

I studied economics at Truman State, then spent five years in market research and managing healthcare certification accounts, and two years as Director of Research at the Kansas City Business Journal.

The rest of my career has been at the Ewing Marion Kauffman Foundation, in two stints (2006 to 2008 and 2015 to now). My work covers entrepreneurship research, labor markets, inclusive prosperity, and the economics of AI. Along the way I earned an executive MBA from Washington University's Olin School, joined the Foundation's core AI working group, and led the Legacy Project.

Most of my work comes down to translation. Researchers and decision makers rarely speak the same language, and I connect the two so research gets used.
